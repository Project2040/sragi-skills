# Tobacco: the 2006 racketeering ruling

**Status:** documented · **Level:** 1 and 3 · **Sources checked:** 14 October 2026

## In short

A United States federal court found in 2006 that the major tobacco companies had deceived the public about the dangers of smoking for decades, in violation of federal racketeering law. The companies were later ordered to publish corrective statements.

## What the sources show

- The United States government sued the major cigarette manufacturers and related trade organisations in 1999 under the civil provisions of the racketeering law (RICO). ([Tobacco Control Laws, case summary](https://www.tobaccocontrollaws.org/litigation/decisions/united-states-v-philip-morris-usa))
- On 17 August 2006, Judge Gladys Kessler of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia found that the companies had violated RICO, in *United States v. Philip Morris USA Inc. et al.* ([SCOTUSblog, 17 August 2006](https://www.scotusblog.com/2006/08/judge-tobacco-industry-violated-rico); [American Cancer Society, 20 November 2017](https://pressroom.cancer.org/correctivestatement2017))
- On 22 May 2009, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit affirmed the companies' liability and much of the 2006 order, and sent four discrete issues back for further proceedings. ([Tobacco Control Laws, case summary](https://www.tobaccocontrollaws.org/litigation/decisions/united-states-v-philip-morris-usa))
- The court ordered corrective statements on five topics "about which they had deliberately deceived the public": the health effects of smoking, the addictiveness of smoking and nicotine, the lack of real health benefit from "light" and "low tar" cigarettes, the design of cigarettes to control nicotine delivery, and the health effects of secondhand smoke. The statements began running in newspapers and on television on 26 November 2017. ([American Cancer Society, 20 November 2017](https://pressroom.cancer.org/correctivestatement2017))

## What this shows

An industry can keep public knowledge about its products' harm unclear for decades, while the cost is carried by smokers, their families and public health. It took a court, years of litigation and the industry's own documents to set the record straight.

## What this does not show

- It concerns the major tobacco companies in the United States, as found by one court. It is not a finding about other industries.
- The sources used here are summaries of the court process from legal and health organisations, not the full text of the ruling. The full ruling is long and is the authoritative source.
