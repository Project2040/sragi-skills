# Opioids: how OxyContin was marketed

**Status:** documented · **Level:** 1 and 3 · **Sources checked:** 14 October 2026

## In short

OxyContin was marketed with claims about addiction that were not supported by research. Companies in the Purdue group, which made it, pleaded guilty to federal crimes in the United States twice, in 2007 and in 2020.

## What the sources show

- **2007.** The Purdue Frederick Company pleaded guilty to felony misbranding of OxyContin "with the intent to defraud and mislead". Three executives each pleaded guilty to a misdemeanour misbranding charge. According to the United States Attorney, Purdue had marketed OxyContin as "less addictive, less subject to abuse, and less likely to cause withdrawal symptoms than other pain medications", without medical research to support these claims and without FDA approval of them. The company agreed to pay USD 600 million in criminal fines, forfeitures and civil recoveries, and the total including the executives' payments was USD 634,515,475. ([U.S. Attorney, Western District of Virginia, 10 May 2007](https://media.defense.gov/2007/May/10/2001711223/-1/-1/1/purdue_frederick_1.pdf); [testimony of U.S. Attorney John L. Brownlee to the Senate Judiciary Committee, 31 July 2007](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Brownlee%20Testimony%20073107.pdf))
- **2020.** Purdue Pharma pleaded guilty to three felonies: one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States and to violate the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, and two counts of conspiracy to violate the federal Anti-Kickback Statute. The resolution included a criminal fine of USD 3.544 billion and criminal forfeiture of USD 2 billion. A separate civil settlement with members of the Sackler family was USD 225 million. ([U.S. Department of Justice, 24 November 2020](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/opioid-manufacturer-purdue-pharma-pleads-guilty-fraud-and-kickback-conspiracies))

## What this shows

Claims about a medicine's risks were shaped by sales interests and were not supported by research. The cost of those claims was carried by patients, families and communities, not by the company's calculation at the time.

## What this does not show

- It does not show that opioid pain medication is never appropriate. Many people need it, under medical follow-up.
- It does not show that every doctor who prescribed OxyContin acted in bad faith.
- It concerns the United States. It says nothing directly about how medicines are approved or marketed in Norway or elsewhere.

## For your own health

Do not change your own treatment based on this. Talk to a doctor or someone you trust.
